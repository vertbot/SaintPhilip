## Our history

St. Philip the Apostle stands at the center of what was a small farming community in Ashford, most of whose families were of Slovak descent. They built the church with their own hands: as they worked their fields during the week, the farmers set aside the stones they turned up, and on weekends they shaped those stones into the walls of the church that still stands today.

According to articles written for the parish's 50th anniversary in 1987, the Diocese of Hartford, which then included eastern Connecticut, sent Rev. William J. Dunn to the new parish of St. Philip in 1921. Father Dunn endured stones thrown through his windows and harassment from local Ku Klux Klansmen who opposed Catholics in the Yankee countryside.

For many years Father Dunn celebrated Mass in the same building where he lived. In 1933, with local engineers and New York architect Paul Chalfin, who kept a summer home in Ashford, donating their services, construction of the church began.
