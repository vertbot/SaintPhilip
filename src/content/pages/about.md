## Our history

St. Philip's story began in 1921, when the Diocese of Hartford, which then included eastern Connecticut, bought a Federal-style farmhouse and about 135 acres on Pompey Hollow Road to start a new parish. Father William J. Dunn, a Connecticut native and the son of Irish immigrants, was sent to lead it. The young parish reached across nine rural towns. For years Father Dunn lived in the old farmhouse and set aside part of it as a chapel, where about a hundred people gathered for Mass.

Those early years were not easy. According to articles written for the parish's 50th anniversary, Father Dunn endured stones thrown through his windows and harassment from local Ku Klux Klansmen who opposed Catholics in the Yankee countryside.

Many of the founding families came from small villages in Slovakia, and they worshiped alongside Irish immigrants and their children. In 1933, with local engineers and designer Paul Chalfin donating their services, work on the church began. Chalfin, a summer resident of Warrenville best known for Vizcaya in Miami, gave the church its copper onion dome in tribute to the village churches the Slovak families had left behind. With money scarce during the Depression, the parishioners built the church largely by hand: as they worked their fields during the week, they set aside the stones they turned up, and on weekends they shaped them into the walls that still stand today.

The church was completed in 1937, and the parish celebrated its Golden Jubilee in 1987. Since 2008, St. Philip and St. Jude Church in Willington have shared a pastor and walked together as one parish family.
